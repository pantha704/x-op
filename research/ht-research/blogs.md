# X Home Timeline Impressions & Original Content Rewards — Blog/Media Research

Compiled for x-op research. Focus: maximizing qualified Home Timeline impressions on original posts,
clearing the OCR eligibility gate (500 verified followers + 500,000 verified Home Timeline impressions
in 90 days, replies excluded), and maximizing the payout unit (unique Premium Home Timeline impressions
with >=50% of the post visible).

Rules of this file: every claim has URL + date + quote where available. Uncertain items marked [UNCERTAIN].

### 1a. "X pays Premium Home Timeline impressions, not raw views"
URL: https://www.versely.studio/blog/x-pays-only-premium-home-timeline-impressions
Date: August 24, 2026 | Author: Versely Team | ~6 min read

Key claims:
- The payable unit = "unique impressions from Premium users (Premium Basic, Premium, Premium+ or
  Premium Business) on the Home Timeline, where at least 50% of the post is visible."
- The payout filter funnel (as a table in the article): Unique (dedupe same user) → Premium only
  (everyone else drops out, "including logged-out") → Home Timeline only ("Search, notifications,
  profile, embeds, a lot of 'it took off' traffic" drop out) → >=50% visible ("Scroll-pasts that never
  occupied the screen" drop out) → not paid/promoted/fraudulent.
- Direct quote: "You are not paid on your audience. You are paid on the subscribing subset of the
  people who saw the post in Home Timeline long enough for half of it to show."
- Direct quote: "That is a smaller, richer graph than 'views.' It is also not a graph you can see.
  X does not break public analytics down by subscriber status, by surface, or by viewport. Anyone
  offering a conversion rate from views to qualified impressions invented it."
- Direct quote: "A non-Premium viral moment is still a follow, still a conversation, still a later
  Home Timeline. It is not this week's cheque."
- Direct quote on format: "If earnings are the question, the definition argues for posts that can
  appear whole in a Premium user's Home Timeline. That is a native, compact, self-contained post —
  not a reply, not an embed, not a 30-image carousel that never has 50% on screen at once."
- Direct quote on video: "Video is a reasonable unit here. A short, specific clip with a first frame
  that reads in the feed is a Home Timeline object. A sprawling thread whose value is in reply five
  is not."
- Promotion does not pay twice: "Promoted impressions are out. Organic Premium Home Timeline is in.
  Paid distribution can still grow the account. It does not write qualified impressions."
- Self-replying / re-quoting to juice repeat impressions is pointless: "Repeats are not unique."
- 500 verified followers / 500k floor is a different counter from the payout unit: "verified Home
  Timeline impressions, replies out, for entry."

### 1b. "Premium Home Timeline needs 50 percent of the post visible"
URL: https://www.versely.studio/blog/premium-home-timeline-needs-fifty-percent-visible
Date: August 24, 2026 | Author: Versely Team | ~6 min read

Key claims (layout/format levers for the >=50% visible gate):
- Direct quote: "A video that autoplays below the fold is not a qualified impression."
- Two failure modes named: (1) "The video is in the post but not in the viewport. Autoplay can fire
  with a few pixels on screen. That is a play, not 50 percent visible." (2) "The post is taller than
  the rule. A 400-word essay with a video at the bottom is a post whose visible half, on a phone Home
  Timeline, is still text. The clip can sit under the fold for the entire dwell. Write less above the
  media, or put the media where the first viewport is."
- Direct quote: "Do not lead with a link. Link cards and quoted tweets add height and steal the
  visible half."
- Direct quote: "Square and landscape behave differently than 9:16 in a timeline card. Vertical video
  in a Home Timeline is a tall object; it can help you reach 50 percent if the card is mostly the
  player, or hurt you if the player plus your caption plus a quoted post exceeds the viewport."
- Production check (verbatim list): 1. Open a draft as it will appear on a phone Home Timeline.
  2. Ask whether 50 percent of this post is on screen without a scroll. 3. If not, cut copy, move the
  media up, kill the quoted tweet, or split the thread so the clip is post one. 4. Confirm the first
  frame reads when the player is only half the card.
- Text-above-media guidance: "Text above the video stays short. One or two lines that name the claim."
  "If the argument needs a thread, put the clip in the first post of the thread, not in post four."
- "For You and Explore are not this surface." Home Timeline is a stream of cards; design for a card.
- Autoplay alone never counts: qualified needs >=50% of the POST visible, from a Premium user, on Home.

## 3. Ordinal — "What 87,000 Posts Reveal" (X analytics benchmark study)

URL: https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/twitter-analytics
Date: Data Jan 2024 – May 2026 (published ~2026) | Author: Francisco Delgado (Co-Founder, Ordinal)
Sample: 87,528 Twitter/X posts. Engagement rate = (likes + retweets + replies + bookmarks)/impressions.

Format benchmarks (avg impressions | engagement rate):
- Thread: 27,508 impressions | 1.89% ER  (direct quote: "nearly 3x plain text and 3.5x video")
- Text: 46,731 impressions | 0.67% ER  (highest reach of any format)
- Video: 30,684 impressions | 0.53% ER (direct quote: "Video posts average 30,684 impressions,
  the second-highest reach of any format, yet the engagement rate is dead last at 0.53%. People
  scroll through video on X. They interact with threads.")
- Image/GIF: 13,053 | 0.99%
- Poll: 3,634 | 0.89%
- Carousel: 12,335 | 0.72%
- Link: 9,573 | 0.68% (lowest reach — consistent with link penalties)
- Text-only vs media (TL;DR): "Text-only posts average 35,975 impressions vs. 18,334 for media
  posts, but media converts harder at 0.79% vs. 0.68%."
- Post length: "100–200 character posts win on engagement (1.09%), while posts under 100 characters
  get more impressions."
- Timing: "Thursday is the highest-engagement day at 1.08%"; Friday 0.89%; Tuesday 0.79%; Monday
  weakest at 0.59%. "Sunday produces the highest average impressions (104,107) but middling
  engagement, making it better for reach than interaction."
- Platform-wide trend: "average retweets per post jumped 35% year-over-year (from 4.93 to 6.67)
  while average impressions per post fell 5%."
- "Overall median engagement rate is around 0.70%; anything above 1% is strong."
- Actionable note: "bookmark count is underrated... high-intent behavior that feeds directly into
  engagement rate in X's calculation. Profile visits are similarly useful."

## 9. Twitterz Journal — "Only 4 to 9 percent of your X impressions actually pay"
URL: https://twitterz.net/journal/verified-impression-ratio-x-monetization-2026
Date: August 6, 2026 | Author: Twitterz strategy team | 13 min read

This is the single most useful piece of growth math found — the "Verified Impression Ratio" (VIR).
- Definition: "VIR is the share of your total X impressions that came from logged in X Premium
  subscribers viewing your post in the Home timeline. It is the only slice of your reach that X
  pays you for."
- Formula: "VIR equals verified Home timeline impressions divided by total impressions, times 100."
- BENCHMARK: "For most creator accounts the number lands between 4 and 9 percent. Under 3 percent
  means your audience is almost entirely free accounts and your payout will disappoint no matter how
  large your reach gets. Above 12 percent usually means a small, professional, high income audience,
  and those accounts routinely out earn accounts ten times their size."
- "That gap is the reason a post with 2 million impressions can pay less than 20 dollars."
- Effective rate: "The effective rate now sits near 8 to 12 dollars per million verified impressions."
  (After the Jan 2026 pool doubling: "Creators have reported payouts two to three times higher than
  2025 on flat impressions.")
- VIR benchmark table (modeled ranges — read as calibration, not guarantees):
  - Crypto/web3: US 11–16% | UK/EU 8–12% | India/Brazil 3–6%
  - Tech/SaaS: US 9–14% | UK/EU 7–11% | India/Brazil 2–5%
  - Finance/trading: US 10–15% | UK/EU 8–12% | India/Brazil 3–6%
- Jan 2026 changes they list: reply impressions stopped counting; "Only Verified Home timeline
  impressions pay. Not profile visits. Not search results. Not notifications. Not logged out web
  views. Not impressions from free accounts."; pool roughly doubled; original authorship tracked.
- [CAUTION] They state "The eligibility bar is 5 million organic impressions across the trailing
  three months" — this matches the pre-Aug-2026 program state, and conflicts with X's Aug 2026 OCR
  announcement (500k verified HT impressions). Treat the 5M line as outdated/superseded; the VIR
  mechanics and rate figures remain the key content.
