a lot of people overcomplicate this: GTM IS MUCH SIMPLER THAN YOU THINK most people hear "go-to-market" and assume it requires complex tools and growth hacks. But no, -> cold email is not gtm. -> content is not gtm. -> ads are not gtm. -> sales is not gtm. they can all be parts of it, but none of them define it. at the most basic level, gtm is: person ➔ place ➔ approach ➔ action ➔ feedback here is what that actually means: 1. person who are you actually trying to reach? not a vague market category like "b2b companies." an actual type of person dealing with an immediate problem today. 2. place where can you reliably find their attention? is it linkedin, x, corporate email, google search, youtube, or an in-person event? 3. approach (channel vs tactic) this is where most people get confused: linkedin is a CHANNEL. founder content is a TACTIC. sales navigator searches are a tactic. direct outbound messages are a tactic. commenting on industry discussions is a tactic. it is the same place, but completely different ways of using it. 4. action what do you want the person to do next? walk through the door, start a trial, book a 15-minute call, or reply with their current workflow? your cta needs to be crystal clear 5. feedback: what happened after you tried? this is the step that turns random promotion into actual gtm. did the right people respond? what specifically caught their attention? where did they drop off? what should change on the next attempt? this loop works outside tech startups too, for a new neighborhood restaurant: • person: people living or working within four blocks • place: google maps and street foot traffic • approach: sidewalk chalkboard menu, local food reviews, and weekday lunch specials • action: walking in and ordering • feedback: did the chalkboard bring more office workers than the lunch discount? the takeaway: you don't need a massive growth stack to start. you need answers to five basic questions: 1. who should care? 2. where can i reach them? 3. what will make them pay attention? 4. what do i want them to do? 5. what did i learn after trying? do it repeatedly and you start building a real go-to-market motion. share this framework with someone who needs to hear it. later today i'll show how that motion becomes an engineering system instead of a collection of random experiments.
